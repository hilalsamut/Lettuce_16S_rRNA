# Microbial Community Changes in Bagged Romaine Lettuce After Use By Date 

This project is based on an extended Romaine lettuce 16S rRNA gene sequencing dataset that was created by Liao and Wang (2022) and Liao et al. (2024).

## Background:
The main drivers for this project:
1. Leafy greens are highly perishable and one of the most wasted commodities - Nearly 70% of leafy greens are wasted
2. Major driver of food waste for leafy greens: Confusion over date labeling 
	- **Use By**: the date after which a product is no longer remaining sufficient quality and should not be consumed, but not necessarily associated with food safety
	- **Best Before/Best if Used Before**: the date after which a product loses the best quality for consumption
	- **Sell Before**: the last day on which a product should be sold
3. Many consumers discard produce past these dates, even if the food remains safe and of acceptable quality
4. Microbial spoilage is typically what limits the shelf life of leafy greens and plays a key role in food loss. 
5. **Research Gap**: Limited knowledge of microbial changes after Use By Date

## Research Questions:
The following research questions were 
1. How does the microbial biodiversity in chopped Romaine lettuce change depending on harvest season?

	- Null hypothesis: The microbial biodiversity doesn’t change with harvest season.

	- Alternative Hypothesis: A rich microbial biodiversity is expected in chopped lettuce harvested in warmer seasons.

2. How does the microbial biodiversity in chopped Romaine lettuce change with storage duration of lettuce at the “Use by date and five days after “Use by date".

	- Null hypothesis: The microbial biodiversity doesn’t change with storage duration.

	- Alternative Hypothesis: A rich microbial biodiversity is expected in chopped lettuce harvested in lettuce samples tested at five days after “Use by” date.
